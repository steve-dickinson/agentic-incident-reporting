# Environmental Incident Response Guidance

## Water Pollution Incidents

### Classification Criteria

**Critical Priority:**
- Oil or chemical spills in drinking water sources
- Mass fish kills (>100 fish)
- Sewage discharge into bathing waters
- Industrial discharge containing hazardous substances

**High Priority:**
- Visible pollution in rivers or streams
- Contamination near protected sites (SSSI, SAC)
- Agricultural runoff affecting water quality
- Reports of dead or distressed wildlife

**Medium Priority:**
- Minor discoloration without identified source
- Foam formation without chemical smell
- Isolated incidents with no immediate risk

**Low Priority:**
- Natural algal blooms
- Temporary turbidity after rainfall
- Historic contamination with no new discharge

### Response Actions

**Immediate Actions (Critical):**
1. Alert Environment Agency duty officer
2. Dispatch field team for sampling within 2 hours
3. Notify water companies if drinking water affected
4. Issue public warning if bathing waters compromised
5. Contact emergency services if public health risk

**Standard Response (High Priority):**
1. Log incident in database
2. Schedule site visit within 24 hours
3. Notify relevant water bodies
4. Check for nearby industrial sites
5. Review recent monitoring data

**Monitoring Actions (Medium/Low):**
1. Add to routine inspection schedule
2. Cross-reference with historical incidents
3. Note for pattern analysis
4. Schedule follow-up if situation worsens

### Required Information

- Exact location with coordinates
- Description of pollution (color, smell, extent)
- Suspected source if known
- Photographs if available
- Contact details for follow-up
- Time and date of observation

## Air Pollution Incidents

### Classification Criteria

**Critical Priority:**
- Chemical releases requiring evacuation
- Fires with toxic smoke
- Industrial accidents with airborne contaminants

**High Priority:**
- Persistent strong odors affecting community
- Visible emissions beyond permitted levels
- Complaints from multiple sources
- Reports of health impacts

**Medium Priority:**
- Occasional odors
- Dust from construction sites
- Bonfire smoke complaints
- Single-source reports

### Response Protocol

**Critical Incidents:**
- Activate major incident protocol
- Coordinate with emergency services
- Issue public health warnings
- Deploy mobile monitoring equipment

**High Priority:**
- Visit site within 12 hours
- Check permit compliance
- Take air quality readings
- Interview affected residents

## Illegal Waste Dumping

### Classification Criteria

**Critical Priority:**
- Hazardous waste (asbestos, chemicals, medical waste)
- Large-scale commercial dumping (>10 tonnes)
- Waste in water courses or flood plains
- Repeat offenders

**High Priority:**
- Substantial fly-tipping (1-10 tonnes)
- Waste on protected land
- Evidence of organized crime
- Waste posing environmental risk

**Medium Priority:**
- Household waste dumping
- Garden waste on public land
- Small-scale commercial waste
- Abandoned vehicles

### Response Actions

**Critical:**
1. Secure site if hazardous
2. Engage specialist waste contractors
3. Document evidence for prosecution
4. Coordinate with police if criminal activity
5. Issue enforcement notice

**High Priority:**
1. Site visit within 48 hours
2. Identify responsible party if possible
3. Arrange waste removal
4. Issue warning or fixed penalty notice
5. Install CCTV if repeat location

**Medium Priority:**
1. Log in fly-tipping register
2. Schedule routine clearance
3. Send warning letter if party identified
4. Post warning signs

## Protected Sites and Sensitive Areas

### Priority Locations

**Special Areas of Conservation (SAC):**
- Highest protection level
- All incidents treated as minimum High Priority
- Require specialist ecological assessment
- Natural England must be notified

**Sites of Special Scientific Interest (SSSI):**
- Protected under Wildlife and Countryside Act
- Incidents affecting flora/fauna are High Priority
- Require site condition monitoring
- May need specialist survey

**Drinking Water Protected Zones:**
- All pollution incidents Critical Priority
- Coordinate with water companies
- Public health notification may be required
- Enhanced sampling protocol

**Bathing Waters:**
- Critical during bathing season (May-September)
- Medium priority outside season
- Must notify Public Health England
- Warning signs must be posted if contaminated

## Documentation Requirements

All incidents must include:
1. Incident ID (auto-generated)
2. Date and time reported
3. Location (grid reference or coordinates)
4. Incident type and classification
5. Description and photographs
6. Actions taken
7. Follow-up required
8. Resolution date
